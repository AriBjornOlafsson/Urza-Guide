I. Overview

This is the 🤖 Urza's Constructs build — 100 cards on the nose, mono-blue and colorless with zero color-identity slips, wrapped around a Tron/Workshop ramp shell that funds an ever-growing pile of Construct and Thopter tokens. It is not built as a combo deck, but running it through a combo checker turns up nine fully-assembled combos already sitting in the 99 (Chapter II), plus a legitimate alternate win condition riding along for free.

Rocks and Tron lands fund an artifact-count-payoff shell (Master of Etherium, Filigree Attendant, Steel Overseer, Simulacrum Synthesizer) riding on top of Tron-style colorless ramp (the full Urza's Mine/Power Plant/Tower cycle, Mishra's Workshop, Urza's Workshop), backed by a genuinely dense five-counterspell suite for protection. Expect to win by grinding into an unanswerable board and closing with an overloaded Cyclonic Rift swing, not by a single explosive turn.

Power level — is this deck actually good?

Short answer: this deck is genuinely strong, not a dud. Checking the exact 99 + commander against Wizards' official Commander Brackets criteria turns up four cards from the official Game Changers list already in the 100 — The One Ring, Cyclonic Rift, Mishra's Workshop, and Mystical Tutor — plus the nine real combos from Chapter II, one of which (Karn, the Great Creator + Mycosynth Lattice) is a genuine mass-land-denial-style lock. No banned cards, no extra-turn effects. That combination — 4+ Game Changers, multiple assembled combos, a lock effect — lands this squarely in Bracket 4 (Optimized): a real step above a typical precon or casual "theme deck," built to compete at tables that expect combos and Game Changers as part of normal play.

The catch, and it's a real one (Chapter VI covers it in full): this deck's power is front-loaded into its engine and its combos, not into raw efficiency or hard removal. Its actual creature base is soft, its only board wipe is a conditional Cyclonic Rift Overload, and it has zero unconditional removal for a resolved threat. Against an equally-optimized table it competes fine on the strength of its lock/combo lines; against a table of clearly weaker precons, it will feel oppressive fast once Urza and a couple of rocks are online (see Chapter VII's note on multiplayer politics) — know your table before you sleeve this up.

This list deliberately skips the symmetric prison-stax package (Static Orb, Winter Orb, Smokestack, Stasis) that a lot of Urza builds reach for — it's generic, it slows down every player equally, and it has no identity of its own. Chapter IX covers why an asymmetric version of that same design space is actually a strong, on-theme fit here instead.

Read this guide in order once, then jump around. Chapters II–IV explain the engine and the lines to look for; Chapter V is the full reference decklist; Chapters VI–VII are for actually sitting down and playing a game; VIII–IX are for changing the 99 later.

II. The Combo Engine

This is not built as a combo deck. But running the full 99 through Commander Spellbook's combo finder turns up nine fully-assembled combos already sitting in the list — not one, as an eyeball read of the decklist suggests. None of them require adding a single card. This chapter is the complete, verified set, grouped by what they actually do.

Infinite mana

Scepter + Reversal C

Isochron Scepter imprinting Dramatic Reversal goes infinite on its own, with no third piece required — you just need enough mana-producing nonland permanents untapped to net a profit. Tap all of them for at least {3} total, pay {2} to fire the Scepter (casting a free copy of Dramatic Reversal), everything untaps, repeat forever. Basalt Monolith, Sol Ring, and Mox Opal are all in the 99 and all count toward that {3} — the more of them you have out, the earlier this turns on.

Monolith + Gadgeteer C

A second, completely independent infinite-mana line: Forensic Gadgeteer's "activated abilities of artifacts you control cost {1} less" drops Basalt Monolith's own {3} untap ability to {2}. Tap Monolith for {C}{C}{C}, pay {2} to untap, net +1 forever — no Scepter, no Dramatic Reversal, no third card needed.

Locks

Karn + Lattice C

Karn, the Great Creator's static ability reads "Activated abilities of artifacts your opponents control can't be activated" — key word control, not own. Mycosynth Lattice turns every permanent into an artifact, lands included. Together: every land, rock, and utility artifact your opponents control goes dead — they can't even tap their own lands for mana. You're completely unaffected, since Karn only restricts opponents. This is the interaction the whole "Constructs matter" pile is quietly one draw step away from at any point Karn resolves with Lattice already down (or vice versa).

Mindslaver + Academy Ruins C

Take an opponent's turn with Mindslaver, then activate Academy Ruins targeting Mindslaver in the graveyard to put it on top of your library. Draw it next turn, replay it, repeat — a genuine recurring turn-lock, not a one-shot.

Drafna + The One Ring C

Drafna's {1}{U} bounce returns The One Ring to hand before its burden counters pile up; recast it for {4} each turn for a fresh "prevent all damage to you, protection from everything until your next turn." Not a true infinite lock (costs 5 mana a cycle), but a repeatable near-unkillable state for as long as you can keep affording it.

Infinite value / storm

Urza + Worldwalker Helm + a copy effect C

This box covers two of the nine verified combos — same result, two alternate enablers: Mechanized Production or Relm's Sketching makes a token copy of Worldwalker Helm plus a Map token. Activate Urza's mana ability tapping a Helm and a Map for {U}{U}; activate the Helm ({1}{U}) to make another Helm copy plus another Map; repeat. Net result: infinite blue mana, infinite artifact tokens, and — since you're generating unbounded mana — you can exile and cast your entire library. This is the single best "just win the game" line in the whole 99, and it needs zero cards outside what's already here.

Drafna + Urza + Sai C

With Mox Opal (a {0}-cost artifact) on board: cast it, Sai triggers for a Thopter, tap both for {U}{U} off Urza, bounce Mox Opal with Drafna for {1}{U}, recast, repeat. Infinite Thopters and infinite storm count for the cost of one extra Mox Opal loop each cycle.

Drafna + Urza + Displacer Kitten + Sol Ring C

Cast Sol Ring; Displacer Kitten blinks Urza off the noncreature spell; Urza re-enters making a fresh Construct; tap the Construct for {U} off Urza, tap Sol Ring for {C}{C}, bounce Sol Ring with Drafna, recast, repeat. Infinite Construct ETBs/LTBs and infinite storm count.

Every line above was independently verified against the actual 99 — not derived by reading card text and guessing. See Chapter X for the exact query. Popularity/complexity data (Commander Spellbook tags each combo with a rough "bracket" — S for simple two-card lines up to E for effort-intensive ones) is in the site's source notes if you want to prioritize which lines to actually learn first: Scepter+Reversal and Mindslaver+Ruins are both tagged S (simplest), the Urza+Worldwalker Helm lines are also S despite needing 3 pieces, and the two Drafna+Urza value lines are tagged E (more moving parts, lower priority to memorize).
Be honest about the ceiling anyway. None of the infinite-mana lines above have a hard damage outlet baked in except by hard-casting Walking Ballista for a huge X — this list has no Aetherflux Reservoir-style payoff. Most games are still won the slow way: a wide board of artifact-count-scaling beaters backed by an overloaded Cyclonic Rift to clear blockers before swinging, with the combos above as insurance/lock lines rather than a turn-4 kill.

III. Gameplan

Early game (turns 1-3)

Rock sequencing: Sol Ring first, always. Mox Opal needs Metalcraft (three artifacts) to activate, so it's usually a turn 2-3 play rather than a turn 1 one, unless your opener already has two other artifacts down. Basalt Monolith doesn't untap during your untap step, but it has its own {3} untap ability built in — you never need Unwinding Clock to make it work, so play it on curve regardless. Tap for {C}{C}{C}, and only spend {3} to untap it the same turn if that nets a real spell; otherwise bank it for a bigger turn 4.

Land priority: this list runs 35 lands, including all four Urza lands and Mishra's Workshop. Urza's Mine/Power Plant/Tower each check for the other two specifically and jump from 1 mana to 2/2/3 (7 mana off 3 lands) once all three are out — that trio is the actual "Tron" payoff and doesn't need Workshop. Urza's Workshop's own Metalcraft ability is a separate bonus on top, uncapped by color restriction. Mishra's Workshop is the single strongest land (3 mana off one land) but that mana is artifact-spells-only — save it for artifact-heavy turns.

15 Snow-Covered Islands aren't just color fixing: Agna Qel'a needs a basic land in play to enter untapped, and Mystic Sanctuary needs 3+ other Islands to do the same — both come online fast off 15 basics. Get Urza down once you have 1-2 rocks so its ETB Construct isn't a 0/0.

Midgame (turns 4-6)

A good turn here is Urza tapping 2-3 idle artifacts for {U} to chain Padeem, Sai, Master Transmuter, or Steel Overseer off Etherium Sculptor/Foundry Inspector cost reduction, snowballing token count. Urza's mana ability isn't rock-restricted — it'll tap a Darksteel Citadel, a Construct, or a Thopter just as well, so a board that looks unimpressive is quietly a mana engine.

Don't sleep on Urza's {5} ability: shuffle your library, then exile the top card, and you may play it free until end of turn. Because it shuffles before exiling, each activation is an independent free-spell roll, and a flood of mana from Basalt Monolith/Metalworker lets you fire it multiple times in one turn — a real late-game value engine, not a gimmick, and a great answer to a clogged or empty hand.

Late game — win conditions, honestly

See Chapter II for the deck's real finishing lines — Walking Ballista off infinite mana, and the Urza + Worldwalker Helm combo as a standalone win condition. Barring either, close with a wide board of artifact-count-scaling beaters (Constructs, Filigree Attendant, Master of Etherium) backed by an overloaded Cyclonic Rift to clear the board before swinging.

IV. Key Synergies

Lines that are easy to miss on a first read of the oracle text.

  • Master Transmuter's actual text is "{U}, {T}, Return an artifact you control to its owner's hand: You may put an artifact card from your hand onto the battlefield." No cost reduction, no "tapped" clause — bounce something cheap and expendable (a Construct, a Clue, Darksteel Citadel) to cheat something big (The One Ring, Mycosynth Lattice, Master of Etherium) straight onto the battlefield for free. It's once-per-turn under normal circumstances — the one exception is Manifold Key, whose {1} untap ability can target "another" artifact and isn't restricted to lands, so it can untap Master Transmuter itself for a second activation in the same turn.
  • Displacer Kitten blink targets that are actually in this 100, triggered off any noncreature spell you cast: The One Ring — blinking it wipes accumulated burden counters, killing the growing upkeep life-loss. Note this does not re-trigger its protection ability — that only fires "if you cast it," and a blink returns the card to the battlefield without casting it. (For a repeatable protection reset, see Drafna's actual recast line in Chapter II.) Karn, Scion of Urza — exile-and-return is a new object, so a low-loyalty Karn comes back at full starting loyalty. Simulacrum Synthesizer — re-triggers its scry 2 ETB every time. Spellseeker — re-tutors a fresh instant/sorcery MV ≤2 every time.
  • Mycosynth Lattice does more than enable the Karn lock in Chapter II. It makes everyone's permanents artifacts, including lands, and lets any mana pay for anything. Under Lattice, your own lands count toward Master of Etherium/Filigree Attendant's artifact-count power/toughness — a 15+ power beater is realistic once Tron is fully online. It also turns Padeem's hexproof and Darksteel Forge's indestructible into board-wide, even land-wide, protection — and stacked with Unwinding Clock, it's one card (Nevinyrral's Disk) away from a repeatable one-sided board wipe; see Chapter VIII.
  • Unctus's loot engine is broader than it looks. "Other blue creatures you control have 'Whenever this creature becomes tapped, draw a card, then discard a card.'" Several creatures here are both blue and artifacts (Etherium Sculptor, Etherium Spinner, Filigree Attendant, Master Transmuter, Master of Etherium, Sai) — tapping any of them for Urza's own mana ability, or just attacking with them, triggers a free loot.
  • Metalworker rewards a loaded hand, not a loaded board: its ability reveals artifact cards in hand, so Windfall refills or the artifact tutors (Fabricate, Inventors' Fair) that put cards into hand rather than the battlefield feed it directly.
  • Urza's own ETB, Karn's -2, and Simulacrum Synthesizer all make the identical Construct token ("+1/+1 for each artifact you control") — every artifact you subsequently play buffs every copy at once, and Steel Overseer's permanent +1/+1 counters stack on top of that dynamic size.

VI. Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • A genuinely proactive counterspell suite (five counters — Counterspell, Mana Drain, Disallow, Refute, Archmage's Charm — one of which, Disallow, also hits activated/triggered abilities), not just a pile of "artifacts matter" value creatures with no way to protect itself.
  • Deeply redundant, largely rock-based mana: the Tron trio, Mishra's Workshop, Sol Ring, Mox Opal, Basalt Monolith, and Metalworker all overlap, and almost none of it needs Urza to resolve first — the ramp plan functions even if the commander is stuck in the command zone.
  • Multiple independent card-advantage engines that don't all collapse to one point of failure: The One Ring (burst), Padeem (steady, if you hold the biggest artifact), Karn (2-for-1 tutor), Forensic Gadgeteer (Clues), Thopter Spy Network (combat-damage draw).
  • Real commander protection, not just hope: Sanctum of Eternity bounces Urza to hand at instant speed on your own turn pre-emptively, Command Beacon frees it from the command zone without cost, Academy Ruins recurs a lost artifact piece, and Spellskite/Drafna's cheap bounce redirect or dodge one-shot removal.

Weaknesses

  • No hard removal for a resolved threat. "Removal" here is Witness Protection and Kasmina's Transmutation (both just strip a creature to a vanilla 1/1) and Imprisoned in the Moon (genuinely turns something into a land, but single-copy and dies to enchantment removal). Nothing exiles a graveyard, nothing answers an enchantment, nothing deals with an already-resolved hexproof or indestructible threat.
  • Interaction is almost entirely on-the-stack (counterspells); there's very little cleanup for things that already resolved.
  • The actual clock is slow: no haste, evasion is limited to flying Thopters/Liberator, and the payoff creatures need real board-development time before they're threatening. The one alt-win (Mechanized Production) is easy for an attentive opponent to answer by killing the enchanted artifact or the enchantment itself.
  • Singleton, and most tutors are narrow: Spellseeker only hits instants/sorceries MV ≤2, and the artifact tutors find "an artifact card" generically — there's no way to specifically fetch, say, Basalt Monolith over a Clue if you need exactly that piece.

VII. How to Pilot

  1. Mulligans: keep hands with 1-2 mana rocks and a land or two over hands built around Urza alone — the ramp plan works with or without the commander in play, and rocks are what let you deploy Urza safely into open counterspell mana rather than naked. A cost-reducer or a counterspell backup is a bonus, not a requirement.
  2. What to protect, in order: Urza first (it's the biggest single point of failure for the blue-mana engine and the Construct plan), then whichever rock is doing the most work (usually Basalt Monolith or Mox Opal). Use Spellskite to redirect removal onto itself, Drafna's cheap {1}{U} bounce to save a targeted artifact at instant speed, and Sanctum of Eternity to get ahead of a board wipe on your own turn.
  3. Sequencing: land cost-reducers before your busiest artifact turns rather than after. Once you're at 4+ lands, lean toward holding up counterspell mana instead of jamming every artifact — the deck's actual clock is slow enough that protecting the engine matters more than raw speed.
  4. Multiplayer politics: this deck reads as harmless through turns 1-4 — a couple of small artifacts and a modest Construct don't look like a threat. That changes fast once Urza and 2-3 rocks are online and the table sees repeated free-spell activations or a Construct board visibly outscaling everyone else's. Use the early window — your counterspells make you a reasonable ally for protecting someone else's board from a wrath, and that goodwill is worth banking before your own board becomes the bigger threat. Don't jam your best mana-sink turn out in the open before you have to — once people notice, you're archenemy.

VIII. Upgrade Path

Pulled against EDHREC's live data for urza-lord-high-artificer — inclusion% is the share of public Urza decks running the card, synergy% is EDHREC's above-baseline metric for this specific commander.

Already running the real staples

  • Sol Ring — 95%/10%, the format default.
  • Dramatic Reversal — 70%/52%, half of one of the deck's infinite-mana lines (Chapter II).
  • Counterspell — 70%/4%.
  • Unwinding Clock — 66%/55%, EDHREC's single highest-synergy card for this commander.
  • Fabricate — 66%/46%.
  • Isochron Scepter — 62%/45%, the other half of that same infinite-mana line.
  • Basalt Monolith — 57%/47%, feeds two of the deck's independent infinite-mana lines (Chapter II).
  • Cyclonic Rift — 54%/22% (Game Changer).
  • The One Ring — 40%/28% (Game Changer).
  • Mishra's Workshop — a Tron-ramp staple in this shell specifically, and also on the official Game Changers list.
  • Mystical Tutor — fetches Dramatic Reversal or Cyclonic Rift to the top; also a Game Changer.

Real gaps vs. the field

  • Urza's Saga — 57%/40%, the single most-played utility land for this exact commander, and it's the namesake land. Absent.
  • Rhystic Study — 57%/26% (Game Changer). No passive card-advantage engine anywhere in the list.
  • Sensei's Divining Top / Whir of Invention — 57%/42% and 55%/44%, both comfortably above-average synergy picks for this commander. Neither made the cut.
  • Rings of Brighthearth — 43%/36%. A direct upgrade to the combo already present (doubles Academy Ruins, Master Transmuter, and every other activated ability at once).
  • Mystic Remora / Mana Vault — 51%/25%, 40%/30% (Game Changer). Cheap draw and ramp staples this exact commander runs constantly; neither present.
  • Pongify / Rapid Hybridization — 35%/-6% and 24%/-10%. Mono-U's actual 1-mana instant-speed removal; the deck's only answers are 3 more expensive, Aura-based (and enchantment-removal-vulnerable) O-Ring effects.

The gap pattern is consistent, not random: this list already runs four official Game Changers (The One Ring, Cyclonic Rift, Mishra's Workshop, Mystical Tutor) and both halves of its core infinite-mana combo, but skips almost every generically-powerful "any blue deck would run this" staple in favor of build-specific synergy pieces. That's a coherent design choice, not a weakness — see the Overview for the full power-level read.

    Both cost exactly {U}, are instant-speed, and — unlike Imprisoned in the Moon / Kasmina's Transmutation / Witness Protection — don't die to an enchantment removal spell once they land. The tradeoff: both only hit creatures (not planeswalkers or other permanent types), and both hand the opponent a 3/3 token (Ape or Frog Lizard) as a consolation. Still a clean upgrade for the deck's thinnest category, just not a free one.

  • recommended
    Add Rings of Brighthearth.

    Direct upgrade to the combo already in the 99 — doubles Academy Ruins' recursion, Master Transmuter's loop, and every other activated ability in the deck at once. 43%/36% on EDHREC; a build-specific synergy pickup, not a generic staple.

  • optional
    Add Rhystic Study or Mystic Remora for a passive card-advantage engine.

    Current draw is entirely conditional (Thopter Spy Network's combat trigger, Padeem's upkeep check) or one-shot (Windfall, The One Ring). Not broken, but a static tax-draw piece would smooth out slow draws while the combo assembles. The two aren't interchangeable: Rhystic Study taxes every spell opponents cast with no cost of its own, while Mystic Remora only taxes noncreature spells and carries a cumulative upkeep cost that eventually forces a choice between paying up or sacrificing it — Rhystic Study is the stronger, less conditional pick of the two if only adding one.

  • optional
    Add Arcane Signet.

    65% inclusion, effectively free — but the deck's existing ramp package (Sol Ring, Basalt Monolith, Mox Opal, Sapphire Medallion, Metalworker, the full Tron cycle) is already strong enough that this is a marginal consistency bump, not a necessity.

One Card Away — Verified via Commander Spellbook

Running the 99 through Commander Spellbook's "Find My Combos" tool (see Chapter II for the nine combos already fully assembled) also surfaces every combo that's exactly one card away. These aren't guesses — each is a real, confirmed combo where every other piece is already in this decklist.

  • recommended
    Add Sensei's Divining Top — already recommended above from raw EDHREC synergy data; a combo check confirms why it over-indexes so hard for this commander specifically. It's the missing piece in seven separate infinite-draw combos already sitting in this list, every one of them built around The Reality Chip (which lets you cast Top straight off the top of your own library) paired with a cost-reducer — Etherium Sculptor or Foundry Inspector — or routed through Displacer Kitten instead. Any one combination you already have in play turns Top into an infinite-card-draw (and near-infinite storm count) engine on its own.
  • recommended
    Add Nevinyrral's Disk — this deck already runs all three other pieces of a real lock: Mycosynth Lattice makes everything an artifact, Darksteel Forge makes your artifacts indestructible, Unwinding Clock lets you crack the Disk every single untap step. The result is a repeatable, one-sided board wipe that only ever hits opponents' permanents — directly answering this build's single biggest documented weakness (Chapter VI: "no hard removal for a resolved threat"). This is arguably the single best card this deck could add that isn't already recommended elsewhere.
  • optional
    Add Rings of Brighthearth — recommended above for doubling Academy Ruins/Master Transmuter; Commander Spellbook adds a third reason: paired with Basalt Monolith alone, it's a standalone infinite-colorless-mana combo, no Scepter or Dramatic Reversal required.
  • optional
    Power Artifact or Forsaken Monument — two more independent one-card infinite-mana enablers off Basalt Monolith alone (Power Artifact is an Aura that reduces its enchanted artifact's activated-ability costs by {2}, dropping the Monolith's {3} untap to {1}; Forsaken Monument triggers an extra {C} every time you tap a permanent for {C} at all, which makes the Monolith net-positive on its own). Redundancy for the combo in Chapter II, not new plans — skip unless the deck keeps getting its primary combo pieces picked off.
  • optional
    Hullbreaker Horror — a legal mono-U 7/8 for {5}{U}{U} with flash, uncounterable, that bounces a spell or nonland permanent whenever you cast any spell. Also happens to combo with the Sol Ring already in the 99 for infinite colorless mana and infinite storm count (bounce Sol Ring to hand, recast, repeat) — a real card on rate even ignoring the combo.
Legitimacy Ruling
LEGITIMATE

The count is exactly right — 99 cards plus Urza in the command zone. Color identity is completely clean: every colored pip in the deck is blue, with zero off-color cards slipping in. And unlike a list that just happens to run good cards, this one has nine real combos sitting fully assembled in the 99, with a real payoff to convert infinite mana into a kill. Two honest problems, neither disqualifying: interaction is thin once something resolves (Chapter VI), and the list skips several of this commander's most generically-played staples in favor of build-specific synergy pieces (Chapter VIII) — a coherent design choice, not an error.

IX. Spicy Alternatives

Symmetric prison-stax (Static Orb, Winter Orb, Smokestack, Stasis) is generic and unfun — it slows every player down equally and adds no identity of its own, which is why it's not in the main 99. But this exact shell is unusually well set up to run asymmetric versions of that same design space, because its own mana comes from artifacts, not lands.

Winter OrbOnly taxes land-based mana. This deck's real mana comes from Sol Ring, Mox Opal, Basalt Monolith (its own separate untap clause is completely unaffected), Metalworker, and the Tron lands feeding rocks rather than needing more lands per turn. Opponents on a normal land base grind to a halt; you barely notice.
SmokestackThe classic answer is "have a renewable token generator to feed it while opponents run dry" — and this list already builds one for free. Sai and Thopter Spy Network keep making Thopters; Karn/Simulacrum Synthesizer/Urza's own ETB keep making Constructs. Feed the tokens to Smokestack, keep your real permanents, and watch opponents' board presence evaporate.
Myr Turbine{T}: create a 1/1 Myr with no mana cost at all, plus a late-game Myr tutor off five untapped Myr. A completely free, repeatable token generator that directly feeds the Smokestack line above and gives Steel Overseer more bodies to pump every turn — and Myr are Mirrodin-native, on-flavor for an Urza shell.
Karn's Bastion{4},{T}: Proliferate. This deck has more counter types than it looks like — Steel Overseer's +1/+1s, the Tezzeret emblem's +1/+1s, Karn's own loyalty, and The One Ring's burden counters. Proliferating is mostly upside, with one honest tension: it also grows The One Ring's burden counter, trading a bit more life loss for an extra card off the same activation.
Whir of Invention{X}{U}{U}{U} instant, Improvise — searches for any artifact card MV X or less and puts it directly onto the battlefield, not into hand. With an idle-artifact board helping pay via Improvise, this is an instant-speed tutor for whatever piece you're missing, meaningfully cheaper than it looks.
A trap worth knowing generally: an {N}-mana "one-sided" board wipe is only actually one-sided if every permanent it hits is a color you don't play. Karn Liberated in this list is deliberately colorless and single-target (−3 exiles one opposing permanent) rather than a symmetrical −X sweeper, precisely because this deck runs real blue creatures (Urza, Padeem, Sai, Unctus) that a "colorless-flavored" sweeper could just as easily catch on your own side of the board.
